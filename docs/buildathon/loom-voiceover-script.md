# Loom Voiceover Script: Lighthouse (about 4 minutes)

## How to record

1. Open Lighthouse-Loom-Deck.pptx and start the slideshow full screen.
2. Start a new Loom: choose "Screen + Camera" so your face stays in the corner bubble. Judges score team quality; they want to meet you.
3. Check your mic, take a breath, and read the script naturally. Do not rush; about 140 words per minute is right. One take is fine; small stumbles sound human.
4. Free Loom caps at 5 minutes, which fits this script with room to spare.
5. When done, copy the Loom share link (set visibility to anyone with the link) and paste it into Q42.

Advance to the next slide each time you see [NEXT SLIDE].

## Slide 1, title (0:00 to 0:25)

Hi, I am Sammarieo Brown. I am a data scientist and software engineer. I work in banking at JN Bank and in payments at Lunipay, here in Jamaica. I am applying with my teammates Raheem Wilson and Matthew Stone as team Project Lighthouse, for Track 04, Climate Risk and Disaster Coordination. And this idea is not theoretical for us. We lived it.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 2, Hurricane Melissa (0:25 to 1:00)

Nine months ago Hurricane Melissa hit Jamaica. Forty-five people died. Six hundred and twenty-six thousand people were affected. A hundred and twenty thousand buildings lost their roofs, and the damage came to nearly a quarter of our GDP. My team and I prepared for that storm, sat through landfall, and spent months inside the aftermath. That is why we want to be in this buildathon.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 3, the paradox (1:00 to 1:45)

Here is what shocked us most. The world's money was fast. Jamaica's catastrophe bond paid out one hundred and fifty million US dollars about ten days after landfall. The IMF approved four hundred and fifteen million more. Donations poured in. And four months later, the Auditor General found that only one point eight percent of donated cash had reached households. The Caribbean does not have a disaster money problem. It has a last mile distribution problem. Nothing connects the forecast to the household, the household to a verified claim, and the claim to a payment.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 4, the Storm File (1:45 to 2:30)

Lighthouse is that missing rail. Every household gets one record, a Storm File, that moves through five states: registered before the season, at risk when a storm approaches, affected when they report damage, verified when agents confirm it, and settled when help arrives. Families interact through WhatsApp, in Patois or English, no app to download. Someone sends a voice note, mi roof gone, mi deh a Black River, and the system turns it into a verified, geolocated claim. The report becomes the claim becomes the payment.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 5, the agents (2:30 to 3:15)

Under the hood, this is an agentic system, and the agents are the reason it can exist. A forecast sentinel watches NOAA and ECMWF feeds around the clock. An intake agent holds thousands of conversations at once. A verification agent checks each claim against the storm's wind field, satellite imagery, and neighbouring reports. A logistics agent matches verified needs to supplies. And the rule that governs all of it: agents do the cognition, the state machine constrains their authority, and humans hold every gate that moves money. We will use the H200 compute to fine-tune a Jamaican Patois speech model, which no other team will have.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 6, why it matters (3:15 to 3:50)

This matters far beyond Jamaica. Nineteen CDEMA member states share the same gap, and none is a big enough market alone, which is exactly why a shared Caribbean platform is the answer, and why nobody has built it yet. Our metric is Time to Relief, from months to seventy-two hours. In five years, Lighthouse is the settlement layer for disaster capital across the region and the wider small island world.

[NEXT SLIDE]

## Slide 7, why us and close (3:50 to 4:20)

So why us? Raheem builds production software at Norus and Intelibus. Matthew is the founder and President of the Jamaica AI Association. And my own background spans banking, payments, and data science. One timezone, the pilot country, and we have already started building against NOAA data feeds. We are continuing after this buildathon no matter what. Because the next storm will not wait. Thank you.
